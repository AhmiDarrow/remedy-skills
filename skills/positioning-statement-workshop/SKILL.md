# Positioning Statement Workshop

## When to use
A product or service needs a foundation for its site, pitch and campaigns.

## Steps
1. List what customers would do if the product did not exist, including spreadsheets, hiring someone and doing nothing. These are the true alternatives.
2. List capabilities the product has that those alternatives lack, verified against the shipped product.
3. For each, ask "so what" until you reach an outcome a buyer would pay for.
4. Describe the customers who care most about those outcomes by situation and trigger, not only by demographics.
5. Choose the category frame the buyer already understands, since it sets which comparisons they make.
6. Attach evidence to each claimed advantage: a measurement, a customer quote used with permission, or a demonstrable behaviour. Mark unsupported ones as hypotheses.
7. Write one internal statement: for whom, in what situation, what it is, key benefit, unlike which alternative.
8. Test it in a few customer conversations before writing public copy.

## Watch out for
- "Everyone" as the audience.
- Differentiators every competitor also claims, such as easy and powerful.
- Publishing the internal statement verbatim as a slogan.
- Describing competitors from memory; check their current pages and date the notes.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Alternatives, differentiators, outcomes, audience and category are written down, and each advantage is labelled proven or hypothesis.
