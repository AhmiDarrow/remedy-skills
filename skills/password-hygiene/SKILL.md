# Password Hygiene

## When to use
A person reuses passwords, got a breach alert or wants their accounts tidied. Never ask them to paste a password, code or recovery key into chat; they type those themselves.

## Steps
1. Ask which devices they use and whether a manager is already in place. A built-in or reputable standalone manager is fine; the point is one they will use.
2. Order accounts by damage: main email first (it resets the others), then the manager, banking, mobile carrier, cloud storage, work, social and shopping.
3. For the manager and main email, use long unique passphrases made of several random words. These are the ones to memorise; let the manager generate the rest.
4. Add stronger sign-in where offered: passkeys or hardware security keys resist phishing best, then an authenticator app, then SMS, which is still better than nothing.
5. Set up recovery before moving on: save backup codes offline, check recovery email and phone, and keep a second key or device. Note how the manager itself is recovered.
6. Replace reused or exposed passwords in priority order, a few per session. Use the manager's reuse report if it has one.
7. After a breach, go to the service by typing its address, change the password there and everywhere it was reused, sign out other sessions, and review connected apps, forwarding rules and recovery details.
8. Record a checklist of account names and status only.

## Watch out for
- Breach emails are a common phishing lure; do not use their links.
- Routine forced changes tend to produce weaker passwords; change on evidence of exposure or reuse.
- Enabling two-factor on a phone without backups can lock the person out.
- Nobody legitimate needs them to read out a one-time code.
- If money moved or identity was misused, they should contact the bank or provider promptly through official numbers.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The email and manager accounts have unique passphrases, strong sign-in and tested or confirmed recovery, as reported by the user, and the remaining accounts are on a prioritised list.
