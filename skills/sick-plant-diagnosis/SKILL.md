# Sick Plant Diagnosis

## When to use
A plant shows damage and guessing a treatment could make it worse.

## Steps
1. Identify the plant, with photos; say how certain the identification is.
2. Ask location, light, watering practice, pot drainage, soil, recent repotting, feeding, spraying, weather and how fast it changed.
3. Check soil moisture below the surface, not only the top. Wilting happens with both dry and waterlogged roots.
4. Map the pattern: old or new leaves, edges or veins, one side or all, spots with halos, holes, stickiness, webbing. Look under leaves with magnification.
5. If roots can be inspected, healthy ones are usually firm; mushy or foul-smelling suggests rot.
6. Give up to three likely causes with the observation that would separate them. Environmental causes go on the list alongside pests and disease.
7. Start with the least harmful action: isolate, correct water or light, remove badly affected parts with clean tools.
8. If chemical control is considered, use only a product labelled for that plant, pest and place, following the label and local rules. Set a check date.

## Watch out for
- Changing water, feed and pot at once so nothing can be learned.
- Feeding a stressed plant with damaged roots.
- Expecting damaged leaves to recover; judge by new growth.
- Plants toxic to pets or children, and homemade sprays that burn leaves.
