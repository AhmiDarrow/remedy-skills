# Travel Health Paperwork

## When to use
Health items must cross borders or survive a journey.

## Steps
1. List medicines from their labels, devices, sharps, liquids and anything needing cooling.
2. For each destination and transit, check the official rules on importing medicines. Some common prescriptions are restricted or banned, and some need a permit applied for in advance.
3. Ask the traveller to request from the prescriber a letter naming generic ingredients and need, and a prescription copy.
4. Refer questions about dose timing across time zones, vaccines or fitness to fly to a clinician or travel clinic, early enough for courses that take weeks.
5. Plan packing: medicine in original packaging in hand luggage, enough for the trip plus a delay margin within legal limits, split only if a clinician and rules allow.
6. Check the airline's rules for oxygen, batteries, powered mobility aids and notice periods.
7. Verify insurance declares existing conditions and covers planned activities and repatriation; record the emergency assistance number.
8. Produce a one-page travel health sheet with allergies, medicines, contacts and policy number.

## Watch out for
- Pill organisers removing labels that officials may want.
- Cooling packs that freeze medicine; check storage text on the packet.
- Undeclared conditions voiding a policy.
- Forum advice used instead of the official border source.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each item has a verified import status or a pending question, documents are gathered and the sheet is saved offline.
