# Portfolio Case Layout

## When to use
Project material exists and needs to become a page a reviewer will skim in a few minutes.

## Steps
1. Ask the target reader (role or client type) and what skill this case should prove.
2. Check permission first: employer or client terms, confidentiality, unreleased work, personal data in screenshots, credit for collaborators. Mark anything needing approval and plan redaction or a recreated example labelled as such.
3. Interview for facts: problem, timeline, team, exactly what the user did, constraints, key decisions, what went wrong, and what happened afterwards.
4. Open with a summary visible without scrolling far: final image, one-line problem, role, team, dates and the main result.
5. Give context briefly: who had the problem and why it mattered.
6. Tell the work through two to four decisions. For each: options, evidence, choice and trade-off, with the artifact that shows it. Cut process pieces that explain no decision.
7. Show the final solution in realistic context, including a difficult state or two.
8. Report outcomes with source and period. With no data, say what shipped, what was observed, or that it did not ship. Separate your effect from other changes happening then.
9. End with what you would change.
10. Draft the layout as ordered blocks with headings, image placement, captions stating the point of each image, and alt text. Check that headings and captions alone tell the story.

## Watch out for
- "We" everywhere, which hides the contribution, or "I" for team work.
- Invented or rounded-up metrics, and personas or research that never happened.
- Unreadable full-board screenshots; crop to the detail.
- A password does not by itself make confidential work permissible to share.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
A block-by-block page draft states role and collaborators honestly, traces decisions to artifacts, sources or omits outcomes, has captions and alt text, and lists items awaiting permission.
