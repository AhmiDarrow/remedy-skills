# Batch Cook Freezer Plan

## When to use
Someone wants ready meals for busy weeks without waste or safety risk.

## Steps
1. Measure the limits: free freezer space, containers, hob and oven capacity, hours available, portions per meal and which dishes the household will eat repeatedly.
2. Choose dishes that freeze well, such as sauces, soups, stews and many fillings. Avoid or adapt ones that split or turn watery: some dairy sauces, raw salad vegetables, fried coatings.
3. Share a base across dishes (one tomato sauce, one cooked grain) and order work so long simmers run while chopping happens.
4. Write cooling into the schedule. Portion into shallow containers and chill promptly within the time limit your food safety authority gives; do not leave a big pot out overnight.
5. Check the safe fridge life, freezer guidance and reheating rule from an official source and record it on the plan.
6. Label each container with name, date, portions, allergens and reheating method.
7. Keep a freezer list that is updated when something is taken out, and plan meals oldest first.
8. Add reminders to move the next item into the fridge to thaw the day before.

## Watch out for
- Loading much warm food at once can raise the freezer temperature around other items.
- Filling containers to the brim; liquids expand, and sealed glass can crack.
- Thawing on the counter.
- Freezing preserves but does not undo time food already spent unsafe.
