# Home Project Plan

## When to use
Work on a home is being considered and has more than one step.

## Steps
1. Define the finished result, measurements, photos, budget, deadline, skills and tools available, and whether the home is owned, rented or in a shared building.
2. Check permission first: lease, landlord, building association, planning and building control, protected-building status. Use official local sources.
3. Screen for hazards before anything is disturbed: asbestos and lead paint in older buildings (get a qualified survey or test before sanding, drilling or removing; do not have the user cut or scrape suspect asbestos to take a sample, and treat it as present until shown otherwise), hidden cables and pipes, load-bearing structure, work at height, damp or rot.
4. Mark what needs a qualified trade. Gas work, most fixed electrical work, structural changes and roof work often have legal limits; confirm local rules.
5. If something is failing, find the cause before planning the finish. Paint over a leak will not last.
6. List materials with quantities calculated from measurements plus waste allowance, and tools to buy or hire. Check product data for drying times, compatibility and ventilation.
7. Sequence the work with inspections, curing times and the points that are hard to undo. Say how water, power or access will be affected and for how long.
8. Budget materials, tools, labour, disposal and a contingency, larger for old buildings. List protective equipment.

## Watch out for
- A stud or cable detector reduces risk but does not prove a wall is clear.
- Ordering made-to-measure items before measuring the site a second time.
- Starting a job that leaves the home unusable if it stalls. Plan a safe stopping point.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Permissions and hazards are cleared or marked as blockers, trade-only tasks are identified, and materials, sequence, budget and stopping points are written.
