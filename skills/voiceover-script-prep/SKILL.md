# Voiceover Script Prep

## When to use
Written copy is about to be spoken and re-recording costs time.

## Steps
1. Get the target duration, audience, tone and any picture timing.
2. Estimate length from word count at roughly 130 to 160 words per minute, then confirm by timing a read.
3. Rewrite for the ear: shorter sentences, the key word near the end, no nested parentheses.
4. Spell out how to say numbers, units, acronyms, URLs and symbols.
5. List names and unusual words with a pronunciation confirmed by someone who knows.
6. Break into numbered blocks matching scenes, each with an ID used in file names.
7. Mark only essential emphasis and pauses.
8. Ask for a short sample read to approve pace and tone before the full session, plus a few seconds of room tone.
9. Check each take against the script for dropped or changed words and log pickups.

## Watch out for
- Tongue-twisting clusters nobody noticed on the page.
- Cloning or imitating a real person's voice without their explicit permission.
- Speeding audio heavily to fit instead of cutting words.
- Changing the script after recording without marking which blocks need pickups.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
A timed read fits the target, every hard word has a pronunciation, and each block ID maps to an approved take or a listed pickup.
