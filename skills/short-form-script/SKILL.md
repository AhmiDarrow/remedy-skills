# Short Form Script

## When to use
One idea must hold a phone viewer for under a minute or so.

## Steps
1. Fix one takeaway, the viewer, the target seconds, who or what appears on camera, and the destination. Platform length limits change, so check the current one if it is close.
2. Set a word budget of about 2 to 2.5 words per second of speech, less if there is silent demonstration. A 30-second clip holds roughly 60 to 75 words.
3. Write three alternative openings for the first two seconds: the result shown, a specific problem, or a surprising true fact. The first frame must make sense with sound off. Start mid-action, with no greeting or logo.
4. Build the body as a chain where each line creates the reason to hear the next. Deliver what the hook promised, with the proof visible.
5. End on one action, or on the payoff itself. A forced loop or three asks in a row weakens it.
6. Lay it out in rows: time range, shot, spoken line, on-screen text. Change the picture when the idea changes.
7. Keep on-screen text to a few words per card, long enough to read twice, centred away from the top, bottom and right-edge areas where app controls usually sit.
8. Supply a caption transcript of the speech, separate from decorative text cards, and a post caption if wanted.
9. Read it aloud with a timer (or ask the user to) and cut whole ideas, not breathing space, until it fits.

## Watch out for
- A hook that promises something the clip does not deliver loses trust.
- On-screen text that differs from the speech forces viewers to read and listen to two things.
- Planning around music or footage without usage rights.
- Paid or gifted content needs a clear disclosure.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The timed table fits the target length, the first frame works muted, on-screen text and captions are written, and any claim needing proof or disclosure is marked.
