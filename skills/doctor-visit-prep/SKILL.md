# Doctor Visit Prep

## When to use
An appointment is short and the person wants to be heard and leave knowing what happens next. This is organisation, not medical advice.

## Steps
1. If the person describes something that sounds urgent, such as chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke signs or thoughts of self-harm, stop and direct them to emergency services now.
2. Ask what they most need from this visit and order concerns with that one first. Cap the top list at three.
3. For each concern record in their own words: when it started, how often, what changes it, effect on daily life, what has been tried.
4. Add current medicines and supplements copied from packaging, allergies, recent tests and relevant history.
5. Write questions such as: what are the possibilities, what is the next test and why, what are the options including waiting, what should make me come back sooner, who contacts me with results.
6. Note practical needs: interpreter, companion, access, repeat prescription, forms or sick note.
7. Keep the brief to one page. Check `calendar.list_events` for the time and suggest travel buffer.
8. After the visit, record decisions, tests ordered, follow-up date, return warnings and unanswered questions.

## Watch out for
- Do not suggest a diagnosis or rank causes, even when asked what it "probably" is.
- Saving the most worrying issue until the door.
- "No news is good news": agree how results arrive and when to chase.
- Health details are sensitive; store only where the person wants.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
A one-page brief leads with the priority concern, and after the visit each next step has an owner and date.
