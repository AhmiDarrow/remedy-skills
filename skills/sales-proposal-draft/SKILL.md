# Sales Proposal Draft

## When to use
A conversation has happened and the buyer expects something they can approve.

## Steps
1. Pull from notes the buyer's problem, their wording, desired outcome, deadline, budget signals, decision makers and criteria. List gaps as questions to ask before drafting around them.
2. Open with their situation and outcome, not your company history.
3. Describe the approach in phases, each ending in a deliverable.
4. For each deliverable set format, quantity, revision rounds and acceptance test.
5. List exclusions and what the client must supply, with dates, and what happens if they are late.
6. Price only from the user's rates or approved figures. Show payment schedule, taxes treatment, expenses and validity date. Check the arithmetic.
7. Use only real, permitted examples as proof.
8. State the next step to accept. Flag legal terms for review and leave sending to the user.

## Watch out for
- Invented prices, timelines, case studies or capabilities.
- "Unlimited revisions" and "as needed" wording.
- Another client's name left over from a reused template.
- Promising outcomes such as revenue growth that the work cannot guarantee.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Deliverables have acceptance tests, exclusions and client duties are listed, totals check out, and unknowns are flagged instead of filled in.
