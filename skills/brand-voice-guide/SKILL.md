# Brand Voice Guide

## When to use
A team needs a shared description of how the brand sounds that a new writer can apply.

## Steps
1. Gather real samples across marketing, product, errors and support, with the owner marking which sound right and which wrong, and why. A new brand needs the audience and a few drafted alternatives to react to.
2. Find the actual differences between liked and disliked samples: person, sentence length, directness, jargon, humour, certainty.
3. Write three or four principles as trade-offs, such as "direct over diplomatic, but never blaming the reader". Words like "friendly" and "innovative" alone guide nobody.
4. For each, give what it means, what it does not mean, and a do and don't pair taken from real material.
5. Map tone by situation while voice stays constant: onboarding, success, error, outage, billing, complaint, security notice. Say what the reader likely feels and where humour is off.
6. Add a word list: preferred term, rejected term and reason, including product names and capitalization. Cover plain and inclusive wording.
7. Rewrite several samples the guide did not use to expose missing rules, and revise.
8. Test it: someone else writes an error message from the guide alone and the owner judges it. End with a one-page summary and an owner for changes.

## Watch out for
- Copying another company's guide or voice.
- A personality essay with no decisions a writer can make from it.
- Examples that invent product claims.
- Forcing playful tone onto payment failures or data loss.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each principle has real before and after examples, high-stress situations have tone rules, and the owner approves the rewrites made only from the guide.
