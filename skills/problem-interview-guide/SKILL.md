# Problem Interview Guide

## When to use
A founder or product manager wants to test a problem assumption with potential customers.

## Steps
1. List the riskiest assumptions: who has the problem, how often, how costly, and whether they already try to fix it.
2. State what evidence would make you drop or change each one, before any interview.
3. Recruit people who recently faced the situation; friends and fans are weak evidence.
4. Open with the last specific occurrence: "Tell me about the last time you..."
5. Follow the story: what triggered it, what they did, what it cost, who else was involved, what they tried or paid for.
6. Keep your solution out until the end, if it appears at all.
7. Prepare neutral probes: "What happened next?", "How did you decide that?"
8. Afterwards record quotes and behaviour separately from your interpretation, and update the assumption table.
9. After several interviews with one segment, check whether new patterns still appear before deciding.

## Watch out for
- "Would you use this?" and "How much would you pay?", whose answers predict little.
- Treating enthusiasm as demand when there is no time, money or workaround behind it.
- Recording without consent.
- Pitching as soon as silence arrives.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each assumption has questions about past behaviour and a pre-stated disconfirming signal.
