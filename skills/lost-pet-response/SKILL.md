# Lost Pet Response

## When to use
A pet is missing, or someone has found an animal that may be lost.

## Steps
1. Get species, appearance, temperament, where and when last seen, health needs and whether it is indoor-only.
2. Search nearby first. Frightened indoor cats often hide very close and stay silent, so check sheds, under decks and garages quietly at dusk with a torch. A running dog may be farther; do not have a crowd chase it.
3. Contact the microchip database, report the pet missing and correct contact details.
4. Call local vets, shelters, animal control and the road or municipal service; visit shelters in person where possible, as descriptions get misread.
5. Draft a poster with a large clear photo, "missing", area, date and a contact number. Withhold one identifying mark to test claimants.
6. Draft local group posts for the owner to publish, and place posters at walking height.
7. Log every sighting with time, place and reliability, and focus on clusters.
8. For a found animal, have it scanned for a chip and follow local reporting rules.

## Watch out for
- Demands for money before the pet is shown, or requests for a verification code.
- Posting the home address.
- Calling and chasing a shy dog that then bolts into traffic.
- Stopping shelter checks after a few days; recheck regularly.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Chip registry, vets and shelters are notified and logged, notices are ready, and sightings are tracked with a next search plan.
