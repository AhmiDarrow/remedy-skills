# Medication List Keeper

## When to use
A person or carer needs one reliable list. The list records what was prescribed and taken; it does not judge it.

## Steps
1. Ask for the actual packets and labels, including inhalers, creams, drops, patches, injections, over-the-counter items and herbal products.
2. Copy for each: name as printed, active ingredient, strength, label instructions word for word, prescriber, pharmacy, start date and what the person understands it is for.
3. Add a separate column for how it is actually taken. Any difference from the label goes on a "raise with pharmacist" list.
4. Flag items that share an active ingredient, or look duplicated after a discharge or brand change, for a pharmacist to check. Do not decide whether to stop either.
5. Record allergies and bad reactions with what happened.
6. List stopped medicines separately with date and who stopped them.
7. Put a "last verified" date and name at the top; produce a one-page printable version.
8. Suggest a medicines review with a pharmacist or doctor, bringing the list and packets.

## Watch out for
- Transcribing from memory or from an old list. Units like mg and micrograms differ a thousandfold; reread against the label.
- Brand and generic names making one medicine appear twice.
- Never advise on dose, missed doses or interactions; refer to the pharmacist or an urgent medical line.
- Outdated printed copies left in wallets and fridges.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every packet appears once, discrepancies are listed for a professional, and the sheet shows a verification date.
