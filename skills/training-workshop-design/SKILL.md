# Training Workshop Design

## When to use
Colleagues must be able to do something new after a workshop.

## Steps
1. Ask what people should do differently at work, how that will be noticed, and whether training is even the fix; a broken tool or unclear process needs repair instead.
2. Survey roles, current skill and access. Split tracks if levels differ widely.
3. Pick three to five real tasks, ordered by frequency and cost of error.
4. For each, plan a brief demonstration, a guided attempt, then a solo attempt on a realistic but safe case.
5. Build a practice environment with fake data, accounts requested in advance and a quick reset.
6. Write exercise sheets with the starting state, goal, checkpoint showing success and a recovery route.
7. Allow more hands-on than presentation time, with breaks.
8. Pilot with one person from the audience, time every exercise and fix where they stall.
9. Prepare a one-page job aid and name who answers questions afterwards.
10. Measure with an observed task and a follow-up after some weeks of real use.

## Watch out for
- A feature tour organised by menus does not teach anyone their job.
- Setup problems can consume the first hour; run a pre-flight check with attendees.
- A satisfaction survey shows whether people liked it, not whether they can do the task.
- Never use live customer or employee data for exercises.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each target task has an exercise with an observable success check, the pilot timing fits the slot, and a job aid and follow-up exist.
