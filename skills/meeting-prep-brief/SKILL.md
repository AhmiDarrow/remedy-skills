# Meeting Prep Brief

## When to use
A meeting is coming and context is spread across calendar, mail, notes and files.

## Steps
1. Read the event: purpose, attendees, organiser, description, attachments. Note if no purpose is stated.
2. Find the last related meeting and its notes, then check which actions from it are done, open or unclear.
3. Search recent mail with the attendees on this topic, limited to what is relevant.
4. Open linked documents and note what changed since the previous meeting, if versions allow.
5. Establish the user's role: decide, present, advise or listen.
6. Write one page: goal for the user; decisions expected with options; open commitments in both directions; three questions to ask; likely questions to them with source-backed answers; links.
7. Mark facts with their source and your guesses as guesses.
8. Name gaps, such as a pre-read you could not open, and what to chase before the start.
9. Offer an agenda suggestion or note to the organiser as a draft only.

## Watch out for
- Mail from threads that excluded some attendees may be confidential. Keep it out of any shared version of the brief.
- Old status from months ago presented as current will embarrass the user; show dates.
- Do not compile personal background on attendees beyond what the meeting needs.
- A ten-page dossier will not be read before the call.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
A one-page brief with dated sources shows the decisions at stake, open commitments and what the user must prepare.
