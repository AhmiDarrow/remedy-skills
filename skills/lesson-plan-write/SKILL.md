# Lesson Plan Write

## When to use
A teacher or volunteer needs a plan for one session.

## Steps
1. Get learners' age or level, group size, length, room and equipment, prior lesson, and any curriculum requirement or access needs.
2. Write one or two objectives with observable verbs: "calculate", "compare", "demonstrate", not "understand".
3. Design the final check first: the task that shows the objective was met and what a good response looks like.
4. List likely misconceptions and prerequisites.
5. Sequence: short retrieval of prior knowledge, modelled example, guided practice with feedback, independent attempt, exit check.
6. Time each segment and plan what learners do in it, not only what the teacher says. Keep long unbroken explanation short.
7. Insert at least one whole-group check where everyone answers, with a decision for what to do if many get it wrong.
8. Prepare support and extension for the same objective.
9. Add materials, a fallback for failed technology, and a segment that can be cut if time runs short.
10. Verify facts and run through activities and answer keys yourself.

## Watch out for
- Asking "any questions?" and getting silence checks nothing.
- Activities can be lively yet miss the objective; test each against it.
- Setup and transitions take real minutes.
- Check licences before reproducing published worksheets or images, and avoid collecting pupils' personal data through online tools.

## Done when
Each objective maps to a practice activity and a final check, timings add up to the session with slack, and materials are listed.
