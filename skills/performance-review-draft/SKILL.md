# Performance Review Draft

## When to use
A review is due and the raw material is memory and scattered notes.

## Steps
1. Get the period, the expectations set at its start, level definitions and the form.
2. Gather evidence across the whole period, from work results, goals and permitted feedback.
3. For each point write situation, what the person did, and effect.
4. Compare with stated expectations, not with a colleague or personality.
5. Check coverage by month to counter recency, and note circumstances such as leave without penalising protected absence.
6. State strengths concretely and keep development points to the few that matter, each with an observable change and support offered.
7. Reread for vague or personality-laden words ("abrasive", "not a team player") and replace with behaviour or remove.
8. Mark gaps in evidence. The author decides ratings and finalises.

## Watch out for
- Inventing examples to support a rating.
- Surprising someone with an issue never raised.
- Health, family or other private matters in the document.
- Using a review draft as discipline documentation without HR advice.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each assessment cites dated evidence against an expectation, development points have actions and support, and ratings are the author's.
