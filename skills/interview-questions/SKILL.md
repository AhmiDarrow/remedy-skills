# Interview Questions

## When to use
An interview will feed content or research. For product problem discovery or job hiring, use the dedicated customer interview or hiring scorecard skills.

## Steps
1. Define what the finished piece needs: facts to confirm, stories, a position or an explanation, plus the audience and length.
2. Research the person's public work and earlier interviews. Note what has been asked often, so time goes to what only they can add. Verify background claims before building questions on them.
3. Draft questions in an arc: easy concrete opener, specific experiences, explanation and judgement, hard questions, then looking back or ahead.
4. Ask one open question at a time, anchored in events: "Walk me through the day it failed." Replace leading and yes-or-no forms, keeping closed ones to pin down facts.
5. Under key questions add probes for an example, a number and how they know it, a disagreement or a changed mind.
6. Prepare challenges fairly: cite the source of a criticism and let them respond. Place sensitive ones after rapport but with enough time.
7. Mark must-ask, if-time and skip, with minutes per section.
8. Add logistics: what the interview is for, recording consent, on-record rules, quote review terms, agreed limits and a backup recording.
9. End with "What should I have asked?", spelling of names and titles, and permission to follow up.

## Watch out for
- Double questions let the guest answer the easier half.
- Reading the list instead of following a surprising answer.
- Agreeing to quote approval or off-record terms without defining them.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The guide fits the time slot, every must-ask serves a stated need and has probes, and consent and attribution terms are covered.
