# Natural Voice Rewrite

## When to use
A draft says the right things but sounds like a template: inflated openers, everything in threes, every paragraph ending on a moral.

## Steps
1. Ask or infer who reads it and where (chat, email, web page). A rewrite without a reader just swaps one generic voice for another.
2. Before editing, list the facts, numbers, names, commitments and hedges that must survive. This is the checklist for the end.
3. Delete throat-clearing: sentences that announce what comes next, praise the question, or restate the previous paragraph.
4. Replace abstractions with the concrete thing already in the source ("improves efficiency" becomes "saves the second upload"). If the source has no concrete thing, ask; do not make one up.
5. Break the patterns: "not X, but Y" constructions, reflexive triplets, a quotable closer on each paragraph, stacked em dashes, "moreover/furthermore" chains.
6. Vary sentence length only where meaning calls for it. Use contractions and everyday verbs if the setting allows.
7. Read it as speech. Rewrite any line the author would not say to that reader's face.
8. Check the rewrite against the list from step 2, then show it with a short note on anything cut that carried meaning.

## Watch out for
- Adding fake personality: jokes, slang, anecdotes or opinions the author never had.
- Removing a hedge that was accurate. "Usually works" must not become "works".
- Promising the text will pass any detector. The goal is that it reads well to people.
- Flattening domain terms the audience actually uses.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every item from the step 2 list is present and unchanged in meaning, and no sentence is left that could sit in an unrelated document without edits.
