# Difficult Announcement Draft

## When to use
A business or community has to tell people about a change that costs them something.

## Steps
1. Settle the facts before wording: what changes, who is affected and who is not, effective date, what stays the same, and what remains unknown.
2. Put the change and the date in the first two sentences and the subject line.
3. Give the real reason briefly. If it cannot be shared, leave it out; do not substitute a flattering one.
4. Tell each affected group what happens to them and what they must do, including the do-nothing outcome.
5. Offer concrete remedies that are actually approved: refund, export, grace period, alternative.
6. Name where to ask questions and confirm someone will be staffing it.
7. Prepare answers to the five angriest likely questions before release.
8. Flag clauses touching contracts, notice periods or regulated data for professional review, then give the draft to the owner to send.

## Watch out for
- Framing a loss as good news ("exciting changes to your plan").
- Apologising for "any inconvenience" instead of the specific harm.
- Promising a date or fix nobody has committed to.
- Hiding the price or deadline below paragraphs of company history.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
A reader of the first paragraph knows what changes and when, each affected group has a stated action, and every promise has been confirmed by its owner.
