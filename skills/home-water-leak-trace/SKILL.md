# Home Water Leak Trace

## When to use
Water is showing where it should not, or the meter says water is going somewhere.

## Steps
1. Safety first: if water is near electrics, a ceiling is bulging, or flow is heavy, have the person shut the main stop valve, avoid the wet circuit, and call an emergency plumber. In a flat, warn the neighbours below and the building manager.
2. Meter test: turn off every tap and water-using appliance, read the meter (or watch its low-flow indicator), wait at least 30 minutes without using water, read again. Record both readings.
3. If it moved, close the internal main valve and repeat. Still moving points to the supply pipe between meter and house; stopped points to inside.
4. For toilets, put food colouring in the cistern and do not flush. Colour in the bowl after 15-20 minutes means a leaking flush valve.
5. If the meter is still, suspect water that only flows during use or comes from outside: shower seals, waste pipes, roof, window frames, condensation. Test one source at a time and check the patch between tests, for example run only the shower for ten minutes.
6. Note whether the patch grows after rain, after use, or constantly. That timing is the strongest clue.
7. Write a record with dated photos, readings and what was ruled out, for the plumber, landlord or insurer.

## Watch out for
- Water travels along joists and pipes, so the stain is often not below the source.
- Opening walls or floors before the cheap tests have been done.
- Heating systems that keep losing pressure may be leaking even with a still water meter.
- Insurers often require prompt notification; check the policy before arranging repairs beyond making it safe.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The leak is stopped or narrowed to a named supply side, fixture or weather path, with dated evidence of each test.
