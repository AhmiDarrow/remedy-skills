# Ramble To Article

## When to use
The user has the ideas, scattered across notes or a spoken ramble, but no shape.

## Steps
1. Split the material into atomic points, one per line, each tagged claim, story, example, evidence, aside or open question. Keep a pointer back to where it came from.
2. Merge repeats, keeping the best phrasing. Something said three times is a clue to what the author cares about.
3. Find the single claim the piece defends. If two compete, show both and let the author choose; the other becomes a later piece.
4. Order points by what a reader needs next, not by the order spoken.
5. Park material that does not serve the claim in a "leftovers" section instead of forcing it in.
6. Draft using the author's own phrases where they are vivid. Write only the connective tissue.
7. Mark logical gaps as [gap: what is missing] and unchecked facts as [verify].
8. Deliver the draft, the leftovers and the gap list together.

## Watch out for
- Filling a gap with a plausible argument the author never made.
- Resolving a contradiction silently; surface it, since they may have changed their mind mid-thought.
- Smoothing strong opinions into balanced mush.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every atomic point is in the draft or in leftovers, and every gap and unverified fact carries a visible marker.
