# Author Voice Match

## When to use
The user wants a draft that reads like their earlier writing, or like the established voice of their own publication.

## Steps
1. Collect three to five samples in the same format as the target. A blog voice does not predict how someone writes a short apology.
2. Note observable habits, each with a quoted example: typical sentence and paragraph length, how pieces open and close, first or second person, contractions, humour type, punctuation habits, words they repeat, how they disagree.
3. Note absences: things they never do, such as exclamation marks, headings or sign-offs. Absences are the quickest tell.
4. Take the content from the user: points, opinions, facts. Voice samples are not a source of claims.
5. Draft, then compare against the habit list line by line.
6. Mark any opinion, memory or promise you had to supply as [confirm] so it cannot slip through.
7. Ask which lines sounded wrong and record those corrections for next time.

## Watch out for
- Caricature: using a signature phrase three times where the author uses it once per long piece.
- Copying whole sentences from the samples into the new draft.
- Writing as a real person who has not authorised it. Decline impersonation meant to deceive.
- Carrying over mistakes the author would not want repeated.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The draft matches the listed habits, contains no unmarked invented opinions or events, and the user has had the chance to resolve each [confirm].
