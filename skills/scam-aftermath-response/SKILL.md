# Scam Aftermath Response

## When to use
Money, data or access has already gone to a fraudster. Speed matters and blame does not help.

## Steps
1. Ask what was exposed and when: bank transfer, card, cash, gift card, crypto, passwords, codes, identity documents, remote access.
2. If a stranger may still be connected to a device, disconnect it from the internet at once. Then money first: the person phones their bank or card issuer now on a number from the card or official site, asks for the fraud team, and asks to stop or recall the payment. For gift cards or transfer services, contact the issuer with receipts.
3. If remote software was installed, disconnect that device from the network. Use a different, trusted device for password changes. Have the device checked or reset before banking on it again.
4. Secure the main email first, then financial and other reused-password accounts: new passwords, sign out other sessions, check recovery details, forwarding rules and added two-step methods.
5. If a phone number stopped working unexpectedly, contact the carrier about a possible SIM transfer.
6. For identity documents, follow the official identity theft guidance, including fraud alerts or credit freezes where available and replacing documents.
7. Report to police or the national fraud body and keep the reference.
8. Build a timeline with messages, payment references and screenshots. Do not delete the conversation.

## Watch out for
- Recovery scams: anyone offering to get the money back for a fee.
- The scammer calling again as the "bank fraud team"; never move money to a "safe account".
- Confronting the scammer, which destroys evidence or prompts more pressure.
- Shame delaying reports. If the person is in despair, point them to immediate support.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The payment provider is notified, exposed accounts and devices are secured, reports have references, and follow-ups are listed.
