# Interview Paper Edit

## When to use
You have timecoded transcripts and need a structure before opening the editor.

## Steps
1. Confirm purpose, target length, audience and what each participant consented to.
2. Check that every transcript carries source file name and timecode.
3. Log candidate soundbites with an ID, speaker, in and out, exact words, theme, and a delivery rating after watching or noting it needs watching.
4. Draft a spine of opening hook, context, tension or change, and ending.
5. Place bites in order. Estimate length at about 150 words per minute, then adjust to the speakers' real pace and allow room for pauses.
6. For every internal cut, write the resulting sentence out and compare it with the full answer.
7. Mark where B-roll or a cutaway must cover the join, and list shots that exist versus missing.
8. Deliver a table the editor can follow plus alternates for weak spots.

## Watch out for
- Splicing words into a claim the speaker did not make.
- Removing the qualifier from a testimonial.
- A bite that reads well but has noise or a look off camera; flag unwatched picks.
- Opening on a line that needs context from minute three.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every line has file and timecodes, estimated length fits, and each splice preserves the speaker's meaning.
