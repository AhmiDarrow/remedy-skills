# Used Car Viewing Check

## When to use
A specific used car, or a shortlist, is being considered.

## Steps
1. Before travelling, get registration, VIN and mileage. Run the official checks available locally for inspection history, outstanding recalls, stolen status, write-off and finance.
2. Search model-specific known faults and service items due at this mileage, citing sources.
3. View in daylight, dry, with the engine cold, at the address on the documents or the dealer's premises.
4. Match VIN on body, documents and history report. Check the seller's right to sell and identity of the registered keeper.
5. Inspect for uneven panel gaps and paint, rust, tyre wear that is uneven, fluid leaks, that warning lights come on at ignition and then go out, and every electrical item.
6. Test drive with proper insurance: cold start, low and higher speed, braking, steering pull, gear changes, noises.
7. Compare service records with mileage for gaps or reversals.
8. Recommend an independent inspection for meaningful sums, and complete payment only after checks, with a written receipt.

## Watch out for
- A warning lamp that never lights may have been disabled.
- Deposits demanded before viewing or sellers who will only ship.
- A warm engine hiding starting problems.
- A recent inspection certificate is not a mechanical guarantee.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each history and inspection item is passed, failed or unchecked, with open items resolved before money moves.
