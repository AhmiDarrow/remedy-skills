# Difficult Conversation

## When to use
The user has to say something they expect to go badly.

## Steps
1. Ask first whether they are safe: any history of violence, threats, stalking or control. If so, do not script a confrontation; point to a domestic abuse service, workplace security or emergency number and plan around safety.
2. Pin down the one outcome they want from this talk, what they can accept, and what they will do if there is no agreement.
3. Split the story into observed facts (dates, words, actions), their interpretation, and feelings. Challenge "always", "never" and claims about the other person's motives.
4. Ask how the other person would tell it, and what they may fear losing.
5. Draft an opening of two or three sentences: topic, specific observation, why it matters, invitation to give their view. Get to the point at once without a long softener.
6. Prepare short replies for denial, anger, tears, counter-accusation and silence. Each acknowledges what was said, then returns to the topic.
7. Choose time, place and medium: private, enough time, not when either is rushed or intoxicated. At work, check whether HR or a representative should be involved.
8. Decide the pause line and how to resume, then offer a practice round with you playing the other person realistically.
9. Draft a factual follow-up note of agreements and next check; the user sends it.

## Watch out for
- A memorised speech collapses at the first interruption. Keep the opening and a few anchors.
- Stacking several old grievances onto one topic.
- Threatening a consequence they will not carry out.
- Bad news presented as open to negotiation when it is not.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The user has an opening, an aim, a fallback, replies to likely reactions and a chosen setting, with any safety concern addressed first.
