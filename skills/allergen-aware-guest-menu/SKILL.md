# Allergen Aware Guest Menu

## When to use
A menu has to be safe or acceptable for a guest with stated restrictions.

## Steps
1. For each guest ask what must be avoided and why: allergy, coeliac disease, intolerance, religious rule or preference. For allergy or coeliac disease ask about trace amounts and "may contain" labels, their emergency plan and where their medication will be. Do not judge severity yourself.
2. List every component of every dish, including stock, sauces, marinades, dressings, spice blends, garnishes, cooking oil and drinks.
3. Check each packaged item's actual label, bought version included, since recipes change between brands and over time. Flag places allergens hide, such as soy sauce containing wheat, or pesto containing nuts and cheese.
4. Prefer a menu where all main dishes are safe, over a single separate plate.
5. Plan cross-contact controls: clean surfaces and utensils with washing, fresh oil and pasta water, separate toaster or sieve where relevant, cook the safe food first and cover it, separate serving spoons.
6. Keep packaging or label photos for the guest to read.
7. Prepare an ingredient card per dish and invite the guest to review the menu beforehand.

## Watch out for
- Wiping a board is not the same as washing it, and heat does not reliably destroy allergens.
- Shared buffets move spoons between dishes.
- Guests bringing food you have not checked.
- Calling anything "safe" or "free from" in a home kitchen with certainty. State what was checked.
