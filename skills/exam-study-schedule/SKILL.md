# Exam Study Schedule

## When to use
An assessment is scheduled and study time needs allocating.

## Steps
1. Collect date, format, allowed materials, topic list and weighting from the official syllabus. Mark unknowns.
2. Read the calendar and ask about fixed commitments and sleep. Count realistic hours, then keep about a fifth unallocated.
3. Diagnose with a short closed-book attempt per topic; self-ratings run optimistic.
4. Prioritise by weight times weakness, putting prerequisites first.
5. Give each topic a first pass followed by shorter self-test revisits at widening gaps.
6. Make each session specific: "solve 8 mixed rate problems closed book", not "revise chapter 4".
7. Mix older topics into later sessions.
8. Schedule at least one full timed practice early enough to act on what it reveals.
9. Leave the final day light, with logistics checked.
10. Propose calendar blocks and create them after approval; revise the plan weekly from results.

## Watch out for
- Rereading and highlighting feel productive but give little evidence of recall.
- A plan that needs every day to go perfectly collapses at the first miss. Say which topics are cut first.
- Do not trade sleep for extra hours.
- If hours cannot cover the syllabus, say so plainly and prioritise; suggest talking to the instructor.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Dated sessions fit the real available hours, each has a testable activity, priorities trace to the diagnostic, and buffer time exists.
