# User Journey Map

## When to use
Problems fall between screens or teams and nobody sees the whole experience.

## Steps
1. Fix the scope: one actor, one goal, one scenario, a start trigger and an end condition. Different user types or goals get separate maps.
2. State whether it maps the current experience or a proposed one. Never mix them.
3. Gather evidence: interviews, observations, tickets, analytics, process documents. Without user evidence, call it a hypothesis map.
4. Draft stages from what the person is trying to do, not from company departments or funnel names.
5. For each stage record actions, questions and thoughts, channels and touchpoints (including offline and other people's tools), waiting time, and information they need.
6. Add feelings only where evidence shows them, with source. Leave unknowns blank rather than drawing a neat emotional curve.
7. Show loops, abandonment, workarounds and failure recovery, and where context is lost between channels or teams. Add internal processes underneath where they explain a problem.
8. Mark pain points and moments that work well, each with evidence, strength and how often it occurs if known.
9. Turn pain points into opportunities phrased as needs, not features. Prioritise by impact, reach, confidence and effort, with an owner.
10. Deliver a table with stages as columns, plus open questions and how to check them with users.

## Watch out for
- Mapping the intended process and calling it the user's journey.
- Averaging conflicting experiences into one that nobody had; show branches.
- A map with no decision or owner attached.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The map covers trigger to outcome for one actor, entries cite evidence or are marked assumptions, gaps and handoffs are visible, and opportunities are ranked with owners or open decisions.
