# Debt Inventory Sheet

## When to use
Debts are scattered or frightening and a factual picture is the first need. No repayment strategy is recommended here.

## Steps
1. Be calm and nonjudgmental. If letters mention court, eviction, enforcement officers or disconnection, move those to the top and direct the person to a free accredited debt advice service today.
2. Gather latest statements and letters, bank outgoings, and a credit report from an official or reputable source if the person chooses to obtain it.
3. For each debt record creditor, account ending, balance and date, interest rate, minimum or agreed payment, due date, arrears, secured or not, and joint or sole.
4. Record promotional rate end dates and what the rate becomes.
5. Mark disputed or unrecognised debts. Do not tell them to pay or acknowledge these; time limits and legal effect vary, so advice comes first.
6. Total balances and monthly minimums, then set them beside income and essential costs as supplied.
7. Flag priority items where non-payment has severe consequences, using the advice service's local definition.
8. Prepare a questions list and a document folder for the adviser, plus a log for creditor contacts.

## Watch out for
- Fee-charging "debt relief" firms that promise to erase debt.
- Collectors who may be fake; verify independently before sharing details or paying.
- Using old balances without a date.
- Recommending new borrowing or consolidation.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each known debt has a dated row, urgent letters are surfaced with a contact step, and unknown fields are marked unknown.
