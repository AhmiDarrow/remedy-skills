# Breaking News Verification

## When to use
The event is hours or days old and early accounts disagree.

## Steps
1. State the current date and time you are working from, and timestamp every item you report.
2. Go first to parties with direct knowledge: the responsible agency, operator or organisation's own channel.
3. Add established outlets with reporters on the ground. Count only independent confirmation: if both rely on "according to" the same account, that is one.
4. Bucket each claim: confirmed by a responsible party; reported by independent outlets; attributed to a single source; circulating without a source.
5. Keep the attribution verbs. "Police say" stays "police say".
6. Treat early casualty counts, causes, identities and motives as provisional, and give their time.
7. Fetch live pages again before answering and look for corrections or walked-back statements.
8. Close with what is still unknown and what announcement would settle it.

## Watch out for
- Search results lag and can surface a similar past event. Check the date on every page.
- Recycled footage and impersonation accounts appear quickly; do not rely on a repost.
- Do not name a suspect or victim from unconfirmed reports.
- Your training knowledge is stale for current events. Use only what you fetched, and say so if fetching failed.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every statement carries a source, a time and a certainty bucket, and the open questions are listed.
