# Recipe Plan Meals

## When to use
Meals for more than a day need to be decided and bought together.

## Steps
1. Ask days and meals, people and appetites, allergies, diet rules, dislikes, equipment, cooking minutes per night, budget and which nights are out. Allergies are hard limits; preferences are not.
2. Ask what is already in the fridge, freezer and cupboard and use soon-to-spoil items first.
3. Fill slots from real constraints: quick meals on busy nights, longer ones on free days, a planned leftover night and one fallback from the cupboard.
4. Reuse perishables across two or three meals so part packs get finished, while varying the dishes.
5. Give each meal active and total time. Link only recipes you opened; otherwise give a simple method and label it as untested.
6. Scale quantities to servings, then merge the shopping list by ingredient with compatible units, grouped by shop section, subtracting what is on hand.
7. Order the week so fragile food is eaten early or frozen; mark thaw days.
8. List prep that saves time, with storage. Cool and refrigerate leftovers promptly and follow national food safety guidance on how long to keep them.
9. State any cost as an estimate unless taken from current prices.

## Watch out for
- Allergens hide in stock, sauces and spice mixes, and "may contain" labels matter; the label on the actual product decides.
- Therapeutic diets, infant feeding, pregnancy restrictions, kidney disease or a history of disordered eating need a clinician or dietitian. Do not set calorie targets for these.
- A new recipe every night is rarely cooked.

Every slot has a meal that fits its time and the hard limits, and the merged list covers all ingredients not already on hand.
