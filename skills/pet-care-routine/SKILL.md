# Pet Care Routine

## When to use
Care must stay consistent across people. This is organisation, not veterinary advice.

## Steps
1. Record each animal's name, species, age, identifying marks, microchip details, health conditions the owner reports and behaviour such as escaping, biting or guarding food.
2. Copy feeding exactly as the owner or vet gives it: food, measured amount, times, storage, treats, water.
3. Copy medicines from the label or vet instructions. Never calculate or adjust a dose. Say what the vet advised for a missed or spat-out dose, or "call the vet".
4. Add a shared log with date, time and initials for meals and medicines so two people do not both give them.
5. List daily, weekly and monthly care suited to the species: exercise, litter or enclosure, grooming, temperature or water checks and equipment.
6. List hazards to keep away. Examples, not a full list: chocolate, grapes, raisins and xylitol for dogs; true lilies and daylilies for cats, where any part, pollen or vase water can cause kidney failure, so call a vet at once without waiting for signs; human medicines for both. For other species, ask the vet.
7. Define call-now signs, including trouble breathing, collapse or unconsciousness, seizure, heavy bleeding or serious injury, signs of overheating, repeated vomiting, swollen hard belly or retching with nothing coming up, suspected poisoning and straining without passing urine. A rabbit that stops eating or passing droppings also needs a vet at once. When unsure, call.
8. Add regular vet, out-of-hours clinic, poison line, owner and backup contact, carrier location, spending authority and house access, shared privately.
9. Keep the sitter sheet to a page or two and walk through it before handover.

## Watch out for
- Home remedies or making an animal vomit without veterinary instruction.
- Running out of food or medicine during an absence.
- Exotic pets whose heating or filtration fails silently; say how to check it.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
A sitter could follow the sheet without calling for routine questions, doses match their source, and emergency contacts and signs are on page one.
