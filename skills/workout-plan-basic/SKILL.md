# Workout Plan Basic

## When to use
Someone wants a general routine. This is not medical advice; people with health conditions should consult a professional.

## Steps
1. Screen first: heart, lung, kidney or metabolic disease, high blood pressure, chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness, pregnancy or recent birth, recent surgery, injury, joint problems or medical limits. If any apply, get clearance or the clinician's restrictions before planning intensity.
2. Ask goal, current activity, experience, days and minutes realistically free, equipment, space and enjoyed activities.
3. For beginners, start with two or three non-consecutive full-body strength days plus easy cardio such as walking. Begin below what they think they can do.
4. Cover movement patterns: squat or sit-to-stand, hinge, push, pull, carry or trunk. Give an easier and harder version and simple form cues for each.
5. Include a few minutes of warm-up with easy movement and lighter practice sets, and a gradual cool-down.
6. Control effort by leaving a few good repetitions in reserve. For cardio, use the talk test.
7. Progress one variable at a time: first repetitions within a range, then a small load increase, only when all sets felt controlled. If sessions keep getting harder or recovery lags, hold or take a lighter week.
8. State stop rules: chest pain or pressure (including spreading to the arm, neck, jaw or back), fainting, severe breathlessness or irregular heartbeat means stop and seek emergency help. Unusual dizziness or nausea means stop; if it persists or comes with any sign above, treat it as an emergency. Muscle pain or swelling far beyond expected, marked weakness or tea- or cola-coloured urine, even days later, needs medical care right away. Sharp, joint or worsening pain means stop that movement and get it checked.
9. Add a log, a short fallback session and a review after about four weeks.

## Watch out for
- Treating soreness as the measure of a good session.
- Heavy lifts near failure without safeties or instruction.
- Weight-loss promises or restrictive diet advice; signs of compulsive exercise or disordered eating call for professional support.
