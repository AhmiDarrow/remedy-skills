# Content Strategy

## When to use
A person or team needs to decide what to publish, where, and what to stop.

## Steps
1. Name the single main outcome content should support and how it is measured today, plus hours, skills and budget actually available.
2. Describe the audience from evidence: customer conversations, support questions, search queries and sales objections. Label guesses as assumptions to test.
3. Review existing content and results to find what already works and what drains effort.
4. Map audience questions to stages: noticing a problem, comparing options, starting, succeeding. Mark gaps.
5. Choose three to five pillars where audience need, genuine expertise and the business outcome overlap. For each, give example pieces and what is out of scope.
6. Pick one primary channel and give each other channel a stated role, based on where this audience looks. List channels deliberately skipped.
7. Estimate hours per format including review and distribution, then set a cadence that fits well within capacity.
8. For each goal choose a leading signal and an outcome measure, a baseline, how it will be collected and its blind spots.
9. Plan a first cycle of about a quarter with experiments, and rules for continuing, changing or stopping each.

## Watch out for
- A calendar of topics presented as strategy, with no choices made.
- Invented personas, search volumes or benchmarks.
- Judging search or newsletter work after two weeks, or only by views.
- A cadence that assumes nobody gets busy or sick.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The one-page strategy shows outcome, evidenced audience needs, pillars, channel choices and exclusions, workload within capacity, and review rules with dates.
