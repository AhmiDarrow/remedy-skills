# Health Claim Denial Appeal

## When to use
A claim or pre-approval was refused and the person wants to challenge it.

## Steps
1. Find on the denial letter the exact reason, reason code, date, appeal deadline, where to send it and whether an expedited route exists. If treatment is urgent, raise the expedited route and the treating clinician immediately.
2. Request the policy wording and the criteria the insurer applied, if not enclosed.
3. Classify the reason: administrative (wrong code, missing information), authorisation, network, excluded benefit, or medical necessity. The remedy differs.
4. For administrative errors, ask the provider's billing office whether a corrected claim is the proper fix.
5. For medical necessity, ask the treating clinician for a supporting letter that answers the insurer's criteria point by point. Do not write clinical claims yourself.
6. Build an indexed pack: appeal letter, denial, policy excerpt, clinical letter, records, call log.
7. Draft the letter: identifiers, the decision, the clause relied on, why the facts meet it, the outcome requested. The person reviews and sends with proof of delivery.
8. Record the response date and the next level, such as external review, regulator or ombudsman, from official sources.

## Watch out for
- A phone complaint may not count as a formal appeal.
- Arguing hardship alone when the denial turns on a clause.
- Missing the deadline while waiting for a doctor's letter; ask whether a placeholder appeal can be supplemented.
- Legal conclusions about entitlement belong to an adviser.

The pack addresses the stated reason, is submitted before the deadline with proof, and the next escalation date is noted.
