# Moodboard Direction

## When to use
People use words like "premium" or "playful" and need to agree what those look like before production.

## Steps
1. Gather audience, context of use, brand assets that must stay, medium, and examples the stakeholders like or reject, asking what specifically about each.
2. Translate adjectives into tensions with a chosen side, for example calm over energetic, crafted over mechanical.
3. Draft two or three directions that differ in underlying idea, each rooted in something about the subject, audience or place.
4. For each give a working name, a one-sentence idea, and concrete traits: colour proportions and temperature, type character, shape and edge, texture and material, light, imagery, density, motion.
5. List reference categories and search phrases (for example "mid-century field guide plates") so someone can assemble images. If you search, record each source link and say which single trait it illustrates.
6. Show how each direction would treat two real items from the project, such as a landing screen and a poster.
7. State risks: legibility, accessibility, production cost, licence needs, cultural associations, how fast it may date.
8. List what each direction excludes, and shared exclusions.
9. Compare against the audience and constraints, recommend one with reasons, and ask the decision owner to choose. Warn that mixing everything produces none of them.

## Watch out for
- References are for discussion, not assets; do not place them in the final work or trace them.
- A board copied from one competitor or artist is imitation.
- Three palettes of one idea are not three directions.
- A written direction cannot be judged like pictures; say images still need collecting.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Two or three distinct directions are written with traits, applied examples, reference guidance, exclusions and risks, plus a recommendation and a requested decision.
