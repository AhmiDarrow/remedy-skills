# Interview Prep

## When to use
An interview is scheduled or likely.

## Steps
1. Get the job description, the CV they sent, interview stage, format, length, interviewer roles and date.
2. Extract the five or so abilities the role depends on and note where their experience is strong, thin or missing.
3. Research from the employer's own pages and recent reliable reporting: what it does, how the role fits, recent changes. Date and link each point.
4. Build five to eight stories from real events: situation, task, what they personally did, result, what they learned. Cover conflict, failure, ownership and the key abilities. Ask for missing detail; never invent numbers.
5. Map stories to abilities so one story does not carry every answer. Keep each to about two minutes.
6. Prepare honest answers for gaps, reason for leaving, "tell me about yourself" and pay, with a researched range if they want to give one.
7. Run a mock one question at a time, ask follow-ups an interviewer would, then give specific feedback on structure, evidence and length. For technical or case rounds, practise thinking aloud.
8. Pick questions that reveal what they need to know: success in the first months, team workflow, why the role is open, next steps.
9. List logistics: time zone, location or call test, documents, accommodation requests. Add a note template for afterwards.

## Watch out for
- Scripts recited word for word sound brittle; use bullet prompts.
- Confidential details from a current or past employer in stories.
- Using assistance during a live interview or assessment where it is not allowed; help ends at preparation.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each key ability has a truthful story or an honest gap answer, at least one mock round has had feedback, and questions and logistics are ready.
