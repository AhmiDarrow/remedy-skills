# Habit Design

## When to use
A behaviour should happen repeatedly without needing fresh motivation each time.

## Steps
1. Ask what the habit is for, when it was tried before and exactly where it broke down. Change one habit at a time.
2. Turn it into an observable action, then shrink it until it is doable on a bad day, such as one page or putting on walking shoes.
3. Choose a cue that already happens reliably in the right place: "after I pour my morning coffee, I will...". Check it occurs as often as the habit should.
4. Reduce friction: set things out in advance, remove steps. For a habit to reduce, add friction, remove the cue, and choose a replacement that serves the same need.
5. Pick an immediate, harmless payoff, such as ticking it off or pairing it with something enjoyable.
6. Write fallbacks as if-then lines for travel, illness, late nights and weekends.
7. Track one yes/no mark where they will see it.
8. Set the restart rule: after a miss, do the smallest version at the next cue, with no make-up sessions. Review after two weeks: if often missed, change cue or size; if easy, grow it slightly.

## Watch out for
- Promising a number of days until it becomes automatic; it varies widely by person and behaviour.
- Streak counters that make one miss feel like starting over.
- Habits involving substance dependence, disordered eating, compulsive behaviour or self-harm need qualified support. Say so; stopping alcohol or some medicines suddenly can be dangerous.

## Done when
There is a written cue-and-action sentence, a minimum version, at least two fallbacks, a tracking method, a restart rule and a review date.
