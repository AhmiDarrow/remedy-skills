# Fact Check Pass

## When to use
A draft's factual accuracy matters before others rely on it.

## Steps
1. Extract each checkable claim with its location into a table, including headlines, captions, names, titles, dates, numbers, quotes, comparisons and implied claims such as "first" or "only".
2. Rank by harm if wrong. Claims about identifiable people or organisations, health, law, money and safety come first.
3. Do not trust the draft's wording or your memory. Find the primary source: the original document, dataset, transcript or official record, and open it.
4. Read enough surrounding text to confirm it supports this exact claim: population, period, unit, definition and whether it shows cause or only association.
5. Recompute percentages, totals and conversions; distinguish percent from percentage points.
6. Match quotes word for word to a recording or original, with context. Check names and current titles at the time referred to.
7. Count independent origins, not repeats of one press release. For superlatives, search for counterexamples.
8. Open every link and confirm it goes to the cited content.
9. Label each claim supported, supported with change, contradicted, outdated, or unverified. Give the source, access date and proposed wording. Leave opinions marked as opinions, and refer legally sensitive claims for qualified review.

## Watch out for
- Search snippets and summaries are not the source.
- Failure to find evidence does not prove a claim false.
- Hedging a baseless claim with "reportedly" does not fix it.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every listed claim has a verdict and a source actually opened or is marked unverified, with corrections proposed and unchecked items stated.
