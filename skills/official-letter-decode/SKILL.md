# Official Letter Decode

## When to use
A letter from an authority, court, insurer, landlord or collector is unclear or worrying.

## Steps
1. Read every page, including the back and enclosures. If a page is missing or unreadable, ask for it.
2. Check it is genuine by finding the organisation's contact details independently; be wary of new payment accounts.
3. Extract sender, reference, issue date, type of document, what is required, amounts, deadlines and stated consequences.
4. Work out when each deadline starts: from letter date, receipt or service. Quote the wording and use the earliest reading until confirmed.
5. Explain in plain words, keeping separate what the letter says, what an official page adds and what is uncertain. For translated letters, note that the original governs.
6. If it concerns court, eviction, immigration, enforcement or loss of benefits, say to get qualified or free accredited advice promptly.
7. List response options only as the letter and official guidance state them.
8. Add a reminder ahead of the deadline and prepare questions for the call or adviser.

## Watch out for
- Treating a reminder or information notice as the decision, or the reverse; appeal clocks usually run from the decision.
- Summaries that drop an "unless" or "except".
- Replying with admissions before advice.
- Phone calls to the sender with no reference or note kept.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The person knows the required action, earliest deadline and consequence in plain words, with uncertainties marked.
