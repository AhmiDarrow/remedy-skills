# Design Critique Session

## When to use
People will discuss a design and the meeting must produce more than opinions.

## Steps
1. Before the session, have the presenter write the user, task, constraints, stage (concept, flow, detail) and the two or three questions they want answered. Say what is already decided and off the table.
2. Invite few people with distinct knowledge and share material early. Assign facilitator and note-taker; the presenter should do neither if possible.
3. Open with a two-minute framing and a walkthrough of the user's task, without defending choices.
4. Give silent review time where each person writes comments before hearing others.
5. Take clarifying questions first, then feedback on the stated questions, one at a time.
6. Ask for each point as: what I see, why it matters for this user or goal, how confident I am. Suggestions are welcome after the problem is agreed.
7. Label each point: evidence (research, data, accessibility rule), established convention, hypothesis to test, or preference.
8. For disagreements, name what would settle them: a test, a data check, a constraint or the decision owner. Park them instead of looping.
9. Include what works and should be kept.
10. Afterwards group notes by issue, set severity by user impact, and record decide, change, test or decline, with owner, date and the reason for declined items.

## Watch out for
- The most senior voice going first anchors everyone.
- Redesigning live in the room.
- Pixel comments on a concept sketch, or reopening strategy at final polish.
- A pasted list of every comment is not an outcome.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
An agenda with goal and questions exists, and after a session each significant point has a label, a decision or test, an owner and a date; unresolved items name who decides.
