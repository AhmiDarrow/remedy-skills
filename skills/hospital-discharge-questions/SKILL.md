# Hospital Discharge Questions

## When to use
Someone is about to come home and the family needs to know what happens next. This collects answers from the care team; it does not give clinical advice.

## Steps
1. Ask the expected discharge date, the ward contact and whether the patient agrees to the carer being included.
2. Describe home honestly: stairs, who is there day and night, what they could do before admission.
3. Prepare medicine questions: what is new, changed, stopped, what supply comes home, and who tells the family doctor. Ask for a reconciled written list.
4. Ask which warning signs mean call the ward, call a doctor or call emergency services, with numbers for nights and weekends.
5. Ask about wounds, devices, diet, activity and driving limits, and who will show the carer how to do any task, with a practice.
6. Confirm equipment, home care, nursing visits, therapy and follow-ups, each with who books it and the start date.
7. Say clearly what the carer cannot do. If home is not yet safe, ask for a discharge planner or social worker before leaving.
8. Collect discharge summary, sick notes and transport, then make a first-week schedule.

## Watch out for
- Old medicines at home being taken alongside the new list.
- Services "referred" but never confirmed, leaving a weekend gap.
- Carers agreeing under time pressure.
- Equipment arriving after the patient.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every question has a recorded answer or named person to chase, and the first week shows services, medicines list and emergency numbers.
