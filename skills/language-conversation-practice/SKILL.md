# Language Conversation Practice

## When to use
A learner wants practice producing the language, not a grammar lecture.

## Steps
1. Ask the language and variety, a rough level with a short sample, the real situation to rehearse and the preferred correction style. Check `memory.search` for earlier recurring errors.
2. Set a role, place and goal with a small complication, such as the shop being out of the item.
3. Speak mostly in the target language at their level, one turn at a time, short enough that they talk more than you do.
4. When stuck, help in steps: rephrase more simply, offer a key word, offer two choices, then translate.
5. During flow, model the correct form naturally in your reply. Stop only when meaning breaks down or they asked for instant correction.
6. After the scene, give at most three priorities, each with their sentence, a natural version and the pattern. Say when something was correct but unusual.
7. Rerun the scene with a twist so they use the fixes.
8. Save the recurring errors and new phrases with `memory.save` if they agree.

## Watch out for
- Long replies using grammar far above their level.
- Correcting everything, which stops people speaking.
- Mixing regional forms or politeness levels without saying so.
- Judging pronunciation from typed text; you cannot hear it. For less common languages or slang, state uncertainty.
