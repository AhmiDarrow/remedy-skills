# Illustration Brief

## When to use
An artist needs enough direction to produce finished illustration or exploratory concept art.

## Steps
1. State which job it is. Illustration delivers a finished image for a placement; concept art explores designs that someone else will build.
2. Describe the purpose: where it appears, who sees it, what they should understand or feel at a glance, and any story or product facts the artist must respect.
3. Separate must-haves, free choices and things to avoid. Include scale, era, function and how the subject is used.
4. Set style with measurable traits: line, shape language, rendering, detail, lighting, palette, with references the user may share and a note on which trait each shows. Do not ask for imitation of a living artist's style or copying a work.
5. For illustration: focal point, composition, orientation, space reserved for text, crops it must survive, and background treatment.
6. For concept art: gameplay or product role, silhouette readable at viewing distance, number of rough variants, then turnaround views, material and colour callouts, expressions, poses or moving parts as the builder needs them.
7. List deliverables with dimensions or print size and resolution, colour space, file formats, layers, transparency and naming.
8. Stage reviews (thumbnails or silhouettes, refined sketch, colour, final) with revision rounds per stage, dates and one approver.
9. Record rights and terms to be agreed: use, territory, duration, exclusivity, source files, credit, portfolio display, fee, kill fee, and whether generated imagery is permitted. These are contract points; suggest legal advice for significant work.
10. List open questions.

## Watch out for
- A mood list with no subject facts yields attractive but unusable art.
- Approving a composition only at final stage makes changes expensive.
- Assuming payment transfers copyright; rules vary and need written terms.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The brief states job type, purpose, constraints, style traits, composition or exploration needs, exact deliverables, review stages, approver and rights points, with open items marked.
