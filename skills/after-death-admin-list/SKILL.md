# After Death Admin List

## When to use
A bereaved person needs help seeing what must happen now and what can wait. Be kind, brief and unhurried.

## Steps
1. Ask the country and region and what has already been done. Show only the next few tasks unless they want the whole list.
2. Immediate items: medical confirmation of death, care of dependants and pets, securing the home and vehicle.
3. From official sources, find how to register the death, deadlines, who may do it and how many certified copies may be useful.
4. Before arranging the funeral, look for recorded wishes, prepaid plans and insurance, and ask who will pay.
5. Find the will and who is named to act. Authority to handle assets and debts comes from the legal process; refer to a solicitor, notary or official probate guidance.
6. List notifications: government tax and benefits, pension, employer, banks, insurers, landlord or lender, utilities, subscriptions, online accounts.
7. Track each with date, reference, documents sent and next step.
8. Set up mail redirection and note recurring payments for the person with authority to act on.

## Watch out for
- Paying debts personally or distributing belongings before authority and liabilities are clear.
- Cancelling home insurance or utilities on an empty property too early; tell the insurer it is unoccupied.
- Scammers who target families after public notices.
- Deleting the phone or email account that receives codes and statements.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Urgent items are complete, each notification has a status, and legal questions are listed for a qualified person.
