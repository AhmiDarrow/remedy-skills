# Event Planning

## When to use
Guests, a place and money have to be coordinated around a date.

## Steps
1. Fix the purpose, date options, headcount range, total budget, who decides, and what must happen for it to count as a success. For a celebration, also ask who is honoured, what they would and would not enjoy, whether it is a surprise (who knows, and the cover plan) and who hosts or speaks.
2. Budget by line: venue, food, drink, equipment, travel, decoration, staff. Include tax, service charges, deposits and a contingency around 10-15%.
3. Gather access needs: step-free entry, toilets, seating, hearing, diet and allergies, children, transport home.
4. Check venues against capacity in the planned layout, not the maximum, plus end time, noise rules, outside suppliers, insurance, permits and cancellation terms. Cite the page and date checked; mark anything needing a phone call.
5. Work backward from the date: venue and long-lead suppliers first, then invitations, RSVP cutoff, final numbers to caterer, confirmations.
6. Keep a supplier table: contact, what was agreed in writing, deposit, balance due date, arrival time.
7. Write the run of show from setup to cleanup with a time, owner and location per line, with slack between segments.
8. Give each serious risk a trigger and a decision time: bad weather, supplier no-show, failed audio or internet, low or high turnout, medical incident.
9. Draft invitations and enquiries for the user to send; the user books and pays. Add agreed deadlines to the calendar.

## Watch out for
- Cancellation and minimum-spend clauses found after the deposit.
- An organiser who is also the only person who knows the schedule. Name a day-of backup.
- Public events, alcohol sales, food sales or amplified sound may need permission; check the local authority.

A budget with contingency, a dated task list with owners, a timed run of show and named fallbacks exist, with unconfirmed items marked.
