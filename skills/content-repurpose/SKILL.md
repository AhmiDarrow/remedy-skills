# Content Repurpose

## When to use
A finished source asset is to be adapted for several channels. For posts about a single announcement, use the social post skill.

## Steps
1. Read or get the transcript of the whole source. Confirm the user may reuse it, including guests, images, music and quoted material.
2. Extract standalone units: a surprising finding, a how-to, a story, a chart, a disagreement. Note each one's location and the context it cannot lose.
3. Check for facts that have gone out of date since publication.
4. Ask which channels the user really maintains, and what each should achieve. Fewer good pieces beat a copy in every format.
5. Give one unit and one job to each piece. Rebuild it for the format: a short post opens on the point, a video script is written to be heard, a carousel has one idea per frame, an email explains why it matters to subscribers.
6. Make each understandable without the source, with the link as an optional deeper read.
7. Check current length and media limits for the chosen channels instead of relying on memory. Add captions and alt text.
8. Create a sheet with piece, source location, channel, asset needed, link and proposed date, spaced so followers on two channels do not get the same item at once.
9. Hand over drafts; the user posts them.

## Watch out for
- Trimming a qualified claim into an absolute one.
- Pasting one summary everywhere with different hashtags.
- Pulling a guest's remark into a clip that changes its meaning.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Each piece traces to a place in the source, reads on its own, preserves qualifications, and sits in the schedule sheet with its needed assets.
