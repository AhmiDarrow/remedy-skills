# Weekly Review

## When to use
At a regular weekly point, or to recover after a chaotic stretch.

## Steps
1. Gather last week's plan, task list, notes, goals and calendar for the past week and next two weeks. Ask which inboxes to include.
2. Walk last week's events and notes for follow-ups, promises and expenses not yet recorded.
3. Triage loose items into do now (only if very short), next action, calendar, waiting for, someday or discard.
4. Review each active project and goal: current state, one next action, blocker. Flag any with no movement for several weeks and ask whether to continue, pause or drop it.
5. Review waiting-for items with the date asked and who needs a nudge; draft, do not send.
6. Look ahead for conflicts, deadlines, preparation time, travel, bills, appointments and renewals.
7. Note what worked, what did not and one cause worth changing.
8. Choose up to three outcomes for next week, check they fit the free time left after meetings, and reserve time for them.
9. Save a short summary so next week's review starts from it. Update goals only with confirmed progress.

## Watch out for
- Doing the tasks during the review so it never finishes.
- Rolling the same item forward week after week; schedule it, shrink it or drop it.
- Marking things done without evidence.
- A review so long it gets skipped; offer a 15-minute version of steps 2, 6 and 8.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Loose items are sorted, each active project has a next action or a pause decision, calendar risks are listed, and next week's outcomes fit the available time.
