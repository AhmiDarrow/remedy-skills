# Learning Curriculum

## When to use
Someone wants to get capable at something and needs an ordered path.

## Steps
1. Turn the aim into something they will be able to do, with a standard: "hold a ten-minute conversation about daily life", not "learn Spanish".
2. Ask what they already know, hours per week, deadline, budget, equipment and how they learn best in practice. Test claimed prerequisites with a small task.
3. Pick a final project that proves the outcome, then work backward to the abilities it needs.
4. Order modules by dependency. Give each an outcome, estimated hours, main resource, practice task and a pass check.
5. Reach a small working result early, and make practice the larger share of the hours.
6. Find resources and open them with `web.fetch` before listing: official documentation, established courses, books, free options first. Note level, cost, date or edition and software version. One primary resource per module with a backup.
7. Build in review of earlier modules and a mixed task every few modules.
8. Compare total hours with available time, keep about a fifth spare, and mark optional modules.
9. Say where outside feedback is needed (tutor, mentor, community) and set a checkpoint to adjust pace.

## Watch out for
- A reading list with no tasks is not a curriculum.
- Inventing course names, links or editions. List only what you checked.
- Estimates that assume every week goes well.
- Skills with physical risk, such as electrical work or climbing, need qualified in-person instruction.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every module has an outcome, a checked resource, practice and a pass check, the hours fit the schedule, and a final project demonstrates the goal.
