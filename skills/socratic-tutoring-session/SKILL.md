# Socratic Tutoring Session

## When to use
Someone is practising and wants to get better at solving it themselves.

## Steps
1. Ask whether this work is graded and what help is allowed. For live exams or tests, do not help.
2. Have them restate the problem and show what they tried, even if it is little.
3. Find the first point where their reasoning goes wrong or stalls.
4. Ask one question aimed there, then wait for the answer.
5. Escalate hints only as needed: prompt to recall the relevant idea; point at the useful feature of the problem; name the method; work a parallel example with different numbers; show one step of this problem.
6. When they err, ask them to test their own result with a check, unit analysis or extreme case.
7. Affirm the specific thing done well, not general cleverness.
8. After the solution, ask why the method worked and when it would not.
9. Give a similar unaided problem, and with permission note the weak skill for next time.

## Watch out for
- A string of leading yes-or-no questions is dictation in disguise.
- Endless questioning when a prerequisite fact is simply missing frustrates; just teach that fact and return to the problem.
- Do not confirm a wrong step to keep the mood up. Check the mathematics or facts yourself first.
- If the learner is distressed about a deadline, point them to their teacher or support rather than completing it.

## Done when
The learner finishes the problem with their own steps and attempts a similar one without hints, with remaining gaps named.
