# Landing Page Copy

## When to use
A single page must explain an offer and lead visitors to one action.

## Steps
1. Get the offer, audience, traffic source, the one action, and what happens after it. Collect price, limits and available proof. Match the promise in the ad or link that brings visitors.
2. Gather the audience's own wording from reviews the user owns, interviews and support messages, plus the alternatives they compare against, including doing nothing.
3. Hero: a headline saying what they get, a line on who it is for and how it differs, and the primary button naming the next step. A stranger should understand the offer without scrolling.
4. Order sections by the visitor's questions: is this for me, how does it work, why believe it, what does it cost, what can go wrong.
5. Pair each benefit with a mechanism or evidence. Use only real testimonials, logos and numbers with permission; mark missing proof as placeholders.
6. Answer actual objections such as price, effort, switching, data and cancellation.
7. Repeat the same primary action where the reader is ready. State near it any charge, renewal or commitment.
8. Draft a page title, meta description and alt text, keeping headings meaningful on a small screen.
9. Check every claim against actual behaviour, flag regulated or comparative claims for review, and propose a five-second comprehension check with a few target readers.

## Watch out for
- Fake countdowns, scarcity or review counts.
- Several competing buttons.
- Headlines that could sit on any competitor's page.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The copy has one primary action, every claim has proof or a visible placeholder, costs and terms are plain, and the user can review it before publishing.
