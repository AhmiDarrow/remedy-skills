# Blog Post Draft

## When to use
A post needs to be outlined or drafted from the user's knowledge and material.

## Steps
1. Settle the reader, what they already know, and the one thing they can do or decide afterwards. Ask for the author's own experience, examples and opinion; that is what a generic draft lacks.
2. Read supplied notes and past posts for voice and for overlap with something already published.
3. Outline before drafting: working title, the opening problem, subheads that state points rather than labels, the evidence under each, internal links to existing posts or pages, and the ending action. For search traffic, use the reader's own words in the title and subheads. If the user asked only for an outline, stop here; otherwise confirm it when the angle is uncertain.
4. List claims that need support. Check them against primary sources and link them; mark gaps `[needs source]` or `[author example]` instead of inventing.
5. Open with the specific problem or result and deliver on the title early.
6. Draft section by section, giving the example before or right after the general point. Show real code, numbers or screenshots where the reader would otherwise have to trust you.
7. State limits and when the advice does not apply.
8. End with one next step that matches the post, then cut anything that does not serve the promise.
9. Provide title options, a short meta description and a list of open placeholders.

## Watch out for
- Invented anecdotes, quotes, customers or statistics in the author's name.
- Rewriting source pages closely instead of explaining in your own terms.
- Padding to a word count or repeating search phrases.
- Publishing: hand over a draft and let the user post it.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The draft delivers the stated promise, each factual claim is sourced or visibly marked, and the remaining placeholders are listed for the author.
