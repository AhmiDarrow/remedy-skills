# Bill Due Date Map

## When to use
Money may be sufficient overall but arrives and leaves at the wrong times. This arranges information; it is not financial advice.

## Steps
1. Read at least three months of statements, plus a year for annual items. List every outgoing bill: payee, amount or range, frequency, date, method (automatic or manual), account.
2. List income with exact pay rules, such as last working day or every two weeks, since these drift against monthly bills.
3. Build a day-by-day running balance for the next two or three months using the opening balance the person gives and the high end of variable bills.
4. Include weekends and holidays that move debits or pay.
5. Mark days the balance falls below zero or below the buffer they choose.
6. Offer options without deciding: ask payees to move a date, keep a separate bills account, switch a manual bill to automatic once funds are reliable.
7. If the shortfall exists across the whole month and not only on certain days, say so plainly and suggest a free reputable money or debt advice service. Contact priority creditors early.
8. Add reminders a few days before manual bills.

## Watch out for
- Annual and quarterly bills missed by a one-month sample.
- Automatic payments on a thin balance can trigger fees.
- Cancelling or delaying rent, utilities or insurance to fix timing without agreement from the payee.
- Treating a credit limit as available income.

## Done when
A dated balance forecast shows each pinch point, every bill has a payment method, and manual ones have reminders.
