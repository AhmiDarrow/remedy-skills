# Sleep Routine

## When to use
Sleep habits are irregular and the person wants a plan. This is general wellness only, not medical advice.

## Steps
1. Screen first. Advise seeing a clinician for loud snoring with pauses or gasping, dangerous daytime sleepiness (especially when driving), acting out dreams, uncomfortable restless legs, trouble lasting months that affects daytime life, or sleep problems tied to pain, medicines, pregnancy or mood. Thoughts of self-harm need urgent help now. If days awake or little need for sleep come with racing energy, seek prompt care. Anyone fighting sleep should not drive or use dangerous machinery.
2. Ask about work or shift pattern, caring duties, current bed and wake times, naps, caffeine, alcohol, screens, exercise and the room.
3. Keep a one- to two-week diary: bed time, rough time to fall asleep, wakings, wake time, naps and daytime function. Estimates are enough.
4. Anchor a wake time that works every day, including free days as far as practical, and choose a bedtime that allows enough opportunity to sleep.
5. If shifting, move in small steps over days; morning daylight helps an earlier schedule.
6. Plan a wind-down of 30-60 minutes: dim light, stop work, set out tomorrow, something calm.
7. Pick only one or two changes from the diary, such as earlier last caffeine, less alcohol near bed, or a darker, quieter, cooler room.
8. If awake and frustrated for a while, get up, do something quiet in low light and return when sleepy.
9. Review after two weeks. For persistent insomnia, mention cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia, delivered by a qualified provider or recognised programme.

## Watch out for
- Strictly cutting time in bed without clinical guidance.
- Recommending sleep medicines or supplements, or changing prescribed ones.
- Chasing wearable scores, which are estimates and can add worry.
