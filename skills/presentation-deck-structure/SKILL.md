# Presentation Deck Structure

## When to use
A presentation must be organised from a goal and raw material.

## Steps
1. Ask who attends, what they know, what they should decide, do or remember, the total slot, question time, and whether the deck will be presented live or read alone.
2. Write the main message in one sentence. If it is only a topic, keep working.
3. Choose an arc for the purpose. Decisions: recommendation, reasons, options, risks, ask. Teaching: problem, concepts in dependency order, practice, recap. Updates: status and what needs attention first.
4. Write only slide headlines, each a full claim. Read them in sequence; they should tell the argument without gaps or repeats.
5. Under each, name the one piece of evidence or visual and its source. Mark missing data rather than inventing it.
6. List the likely objections and answer the decisive ones in the main line, others in an appendix.
7. Allocate minutes by importance, leave margin for interruptions, and mark slides to skip if cut short. Do not rely on a fixed slides-per-minute rule.
8. Draft speaker notes with the point, example and transition. For a read-alone deck, put necessary explanation on the page.
9. Plan a timed rehearsal aloud and a fallback for demos or video.

## Watch out for
- Narrating work in the order it was done, so the answer arrives last.
- An agenda and company history using the first quarter of the time.
- Ending on "Questions?" without restating the ask.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The headline sequence conveys the argument, each slide has one supporting element or a marked gap, timing fits with question time, and the ask is explicit.
