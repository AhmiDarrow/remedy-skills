# Plain Language Rewrite

## When to use
A notice, policy summary, form instruction or specialist explanation is going to readers outside the field.

## Steps
1. Name the reader and what they must do after reading. Order the whole text around those actions.
2. Mark every "must", "may", "should", "unless", "only if", deadline, amount and exception in the source. These are the legal and practical skeleton.
3. Address the reader as "you" and name who does each action; convert passive duties into "we will" or "you must".
4. Turn noun phrases back into verbs ("make an application" to "apply").
5. Put one condition per sentence. For three or more conditions use a list that says plainly whether all or any apply.
6. Pick one word per concept and keep it. Define unavoidable terms where they first appear.
7. Add headings phrased as the reader's questions.
8. Map each marked item from step 2 to its new location in a two-column table and flag anything you could not rewrite with certainty.

## Watch out for
- Changing "may" into "will", or merging "and" with "or" in eligibility lists.
- Treating a readability score as proof. Common formulas count surface features such as word and sentence length, not comprehension; use them only to find hard spots.
- Presenting a plain version of a contract or law as legally equivalent. Say the original governs and have a qualified person review it.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The mapping table accounts for every obligation, condition and date, and each action the reader must take appears under its own heading or step.
