# Headline Options

## When to use
Finished or drafted content needs a title or an opening that earns attention honestly.

## Steps
1. Read the actual piece. Write down its most specific payoff, who it is for, and the proof it contains. If it has no clear payoff, say so.
2. Ask where the line appears: search result, feed, video, inbox or page. Search needs the reader's words near the front; a feed or inbox line needs a reason to stop; a video title must work with its thumbnail without repeating it.
3. Collect useful raw material from the piece: concrete numbers, the mistake it prevents, the surprising finding, the reader's own phrasing.
4. Write about ten options using different angles, such as direct outcome, problem, question, how-to, contrast, finding and short story opening. Make them differ in idea, not word order.
5. For spoken or video hooks, write the first sentences so the topic and stake arrive immediately.
6. Test each against the content: does the piece fully deliver, and would a reader feel misled afterwards? Remove failures and unsupported superlatives.
7. Preview the finalists as they will be truncated; check current platform limits if they matter.
8. Recommend the top two or three, each with the angle, intended reader and trade-off. If the user has enough traffic to test, suggest comparing them on a measure beyond clicks.

## Watch out for
- Curiosity gaps that withhold the very thing the title promises.
- Invented numbers or urgency.
- Insider terms the target reader would not search or recognise.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The shortlist contains distinct options, each fully supported by the piece, fitted to its placement, with a reason for the recommendation.
