# Scope Change Assessment

## When to use
New or altered work is requested after scope was agreed.

## Steps
1. Write the request in the requester's words and the problem behind it.
2. Compare with the agreed scope document, quoting the relevant lines. Decide whether it is in scope, a clarification, or a change; mark ambiguity honestly.
3. Ask the people doing the work for effort, including rework of finished items, testing, docs and training.
4. Trace effect on the schedule: does it touch the critical path or a person already fully booked?
5. State cost and who pays, based on the agreement's change terms.
6. Offer options: accept with new date or fee, swap for equal work, defer to a later phase, decline.
7. State the cost of deciding late and a decision-by date.
8. Record the decision and approver, then update scope, plan and price together. Work starts after written approval.

## Watch out for
- Absorbing "small" changes untracked until the date slips.
- Quoting only build effort.
- Reading contract terms as settled when disputed; get qualified advice.
- Verbal approval from someone without budget authority.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The requester can choose among options with stated schedule and cost effects, and any approval is recorded with its approver.
