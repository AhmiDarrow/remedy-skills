# Elder Home Safety Walkthrough

## When to use
A family wants to make a home safer while respecting the resident's choices.

## Steps
1. Ask the resident first what feels difficult or has gone wrong, including near-misses. Get agreement to the walkthrough.
2. Follow a day: bed, toilet at night, washing, dressing, kitchen, stairs, front door, bins, outdoors.
3. At each point check lighting and switch reach, loose rugs and cables, thresholds, handrails, slippery surfaces, seat and bed height, and items needing a step to reach.
4. Check smoke and CO alarms, whether they can be heard, cooker use, heaters near fabric, blocked exits and hot water temperature.
5. Check that help can be called from the floor and that a trusted person or emergency crew could get in.
6. After a fall, new unsteadiness, dizziness or memory change, recommend a clinician; ask about an occupational therapist home assessment for equipment and grab rails.
7. Rank findings by likely harm and ease: fix today, buy soon, needs installer or assessment.
8. Agree each change with the resident and record what they decline.

## Watch out for
- Towel rails or suction handles used as grab bars.
- Rearranging furniture so familiar paths and handholds vanish.
- Imposing cameras or sensors without informed consent.
- Fitting equipment without correct height or wall fixing.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
A ranked list with owner and date exists, today's items are done, and declined items are recorded respectfully.
