# Case Study Write

## When to use
Completed work should be told as evidence of what was done and what changed.

## Steps
1. Establish permission first: may the customer be named, who approves, and what is confidential. Without permission, ask whether an anonymized version is allowed; removing a name may not hide identity.
2. Collect source material: interview notes, proposal, messages, before and after measurements and approved quotes.
3. Record each result with baseline, later value, period, measurement method and source.
4. Check attribution. List other changes in the same period such as season, campaigns or staffing, and claim only what the evidence supports. With no numbers, report observed changes plainly.
5. Build the story: situation and stakes, constraints, what was tried before, the approach with one real decision or setback, results and what remains unsolved.
6. Put the strongest verified result and who it applies to at the top, with a short summary box.
7. Use quotes only as actually said. If a suggested quote is drafted, label it as pending until the speaker approves the exact wording.
8. Add a next step relevant to a reader in the same situation.
9. Deliver with an approval sheet listing each name, number, quote and logo with its status. The user sends it for sign-off and publishes.

## Watch out for
- Percentages from tiny bases without the absolute numbers.
- Turning one customer's outcome into a promise.
- Your product as the hero of every sentence while the customer's work disappears.
- Screenshots exposing personal or internal data.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every number and quote traces to a source, unapproved items are clearly marked, and nothing confidential remains in the draft.
