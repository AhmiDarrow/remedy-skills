# Privacy Checkup

## When to use
A person wants to review their own exposure on their own accounts and devices.

## Steps
1. Ask what worries them and from whom: advertisers, strangers, an employer or someone close. If it is an abusive, controlling or stalking partner, relative or other person, stop: removing trackers or access can alert that person and raise risk. Direct them to a specialist support service from a device the other person cannot reach.
2. Start with primary email, phone platform account, password manager, banking and social accounts, since email controls resets.
3. On each, review signed-in devices, connected third-party apps, forwarding rules, recovery details and sign-in protection.
4. On the phone, review location (including precise and background), camera, microphone, contacts, photos, accessibility and device-admin access, and keyboards. Remove unused apps.
5. Review who can see what: shared albums, drive links, calendars, family sharing, location sharing, profile visibility, discovery by phone or email, old posts.
6. Check ad personalisation, activity history and auto-delete, lock screen previews and photo location metadata when sharing.
7. Find current menu paths in official help, since they move.
8. The user changes settings and types any passwords or codes. Record each change and what it might break.
9. For data copies or deletion requests, use the service's official page; keep downloaded archives somewhere protected. Set a next review.

## Watch out for
- Deleting an account before saving needed data or moving logins that depend on it.
- Revoking access that backs up photos or runs two-step sign-in.
- Claiming a setting stops all tracking; private browsing and ad opt-outs have limits.
- Writing passwords or recovery codes into the checklist.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Priority accounts and phone permissions are marked reviewed or pending, changes are logged, and remaining exposures are stated plainly.
