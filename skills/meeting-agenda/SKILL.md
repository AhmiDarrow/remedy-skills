# Meeting Agenda

## When to use
A meeting needs to produce something and has limited time.

## Steps
1. Write one sentence: "By the end we will have..." If nothing needs live discussion, suggest a written update instead.
2. Collect topics, the meeting length, attendees and open actions from the last notes.
3. For each topic state the outcome type (decide, generate options, solve, inform), the question to answer, who leads it and what must be read first.
4. Phrase items as questions, such as "Which date do we launch?", so the group can tell when it is done.
5. For decisions, name who decides and by what method, and make sure that person is invited.
6. Order by importance, not habit, so the item that cannot slip is not last. Time each, leaving about ten percent spare plus five minutes to confirm decisions, actions and owners.
7. If items do not fit, cut or move them rather than shrinking every slot.
8. Check each attendee is needed for some item; mark others optional or send them notes.
9. Draft the agenda with goal, pre-reads, roles (chair, note taker) and what to prepare. The user sends it, early enough to allow reading.

## Watch out for
- Round-robin status reports eating the time meant for decisions.
- Five-minute slots for contentious topics.
- Pre-reads linked without access permission or sent the same hour.
- No place for new issues; note them for later instead of letting them take over.

## Done when
Each item has a question, outcome type, lead and duration, the total fits with spare time, and deciders are on the invite.
