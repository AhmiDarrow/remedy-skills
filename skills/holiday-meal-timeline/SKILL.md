# Holiday Meal Timeline

## When to use
Several dishes must land on the table together in one ordinary kitchen.

## Steps
1. Fix serving time, guest count, dishes, and equipment: ovens and shelves, burners, small appliances, fridge and counter space.
2. For each dish record active time, passive time, oven temperature, vessel, whether it holds warm, reheats or must be served at once.
3. Find collisions: two dishes needing the oven at different temperatures at once, or more pans than burners. Move one earlier, to the hob or another appliance, or swap the dish.
4. If meat or fish is frozen, schedule fridge thawing first, using the official guidance for its weight; it can take several days.
5. Plan backwards from serving. Put resting time for the roast in the schedule, and use that window for oven-temperature dishes.
6. Spread work: two days before (shopping, sauces that keep), day before (chopping, desserts, setting the table), day of.
7. Write a clock-time run sheet with one action per line and who does it, leaving slack of 30 minutes for the main roast.
8. Add a finish checklist: serving dishes assigned, drinks, dietary labels, and a leftover plan to chill food within the food safety authority's time limit.

## Watch out for
- A crowded oven slows everything.
- Roast time per weight is an estimate; the thermometer reading decides.
- Dish space for chilled items runs out first; plan a safe cold spot, not a hope that outdoors is cold enough.
- Scheduling the cook to do two active tasks at once.

A timed run sheet has no oven, burner or person double-booked and includes thawing, resting and chilling.
