# Contract Plain Language Walkthrough

## When to use
A lease, service agreement or terms of use needs to be understood. This is reading help, not legal advice.

## Steps
1. Say up front that you can explain wording and organise questions but cannot judge enforceability or what the law allows.
2. Confirm completeness: all pages, schedules, exhibits and any documents incorporated by reference or link. List the missing ones.
3. Extract the defined terms first; capitalised words often mean something narrower or wider than usual.
4. Build a table with clause number: parties, term and renewal, notice periods and method, payments and fees, termination, liability and indemnity, ownership of work or data, restrictions after the end, disputes and governing law, change-of-terms rights.
5. Restate each obligation as who must do what, by when, and what happens otherwise.
6. Trace cross-references and phrases such as "notwithstanding" and "subject to" to see which clause wins on the wording.
7. Flag one-sided, blank, vague or internally inconsistent items as questions, quoting the clause.
8. Compare with what the user believes was agreed and list the differences.
9. Recommend a lawyer or advice service where stakes are significant or a term is unclear.

## Watch out for
- A summary never replaces the text; keep clause numbers so the user can read the originals.
- Do not call a clause standard, safe or unenforceable.
- Auto-renewal and notice windows are easy to miss; calculate the dates and show the working.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Obligations, deadlines and open questions are listed against clause numbers, with missing attachments and the limits of this reading stated.
