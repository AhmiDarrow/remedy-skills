# Meeting Notes

## When to use
Raw material from a meeting needs to become a record people will act on.

## Steps
1. Read all of it first; later remarks often reverse earlier ones. Ask who the notes are for and whether a formal format is required.
2. Record title, date, attendees and absences only as supported by the material.
3. Mark something decided only when there is clear agreement or the decider says so. "We could", "I think we should" and silence stay proposals.
4. For each action capture the task, one owner, due date and source line. Write "owner not assigned" or "no date" when missing; do not infer from who was talking.
5. Convert "Friday" or "next week" to a date only when the meeting date makes it certain; otherwise quote the phrase.
6. List open questions, disagreements and risks, with who is following up if said.
7. Summarise discussion by topic, only enough to explain decisions. Quote sparingly and exactly.
8. List uncertainties: unclear speaker, garbled names or numbers, inaudible parts. Verify figures against any shared document.
9. Put decisions and actions first, show the draft, and let the user choose recipients and send it.

## Watch out for
- Automatic transcripts often drop "not" and mix up speakers, names and numbers; check any line a decision rests on.
- Side remarks, personal or health information and off-record comments do not belong in shared notes. Flag them.
- Smoothing disagreement into consensus.
- Adding actions that seem sensible but nobody agreed to; offer them separately as suggestions.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
Every decision and action traces to the source, missing owners or dates are visible, and uncertain passages are listed for confirmation.
