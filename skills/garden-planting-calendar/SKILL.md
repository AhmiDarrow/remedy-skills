# Garden Planting Calendar

## When to use
Someone wants to grow food or flowers and needs dates that fit their location.

## Steps
1. Ask location to town or region level, altitude if notable, and hemisphere.
2. Find average last spring and first autumn frost dates, or wet and dry seasons in frost-free places, from a weather service or extension body. Treat them as probabilities.
3. Observe the site: hours of sun by area, wind, soil drainage, containers or beds, water access.
4. List crops the household will actually use and check each fits sun, space and season length.
5. For each, take from the packet or a local source: sow indoors, transplant, direct sow, spacing and days to harvest. Compute dates relative to frost dates, and check days to harvest fit the season.
6. Stagger repeat sowings for crops that ripen all at once.
7. Plan rotation by plant family for beds used before.
8. Add hardening-off periods and reminders worded with conditions, such as "if night forecast is mild".

## Watch out for
- Calendars written for another climate.
- Starting seedlings indoors too early without enough light.
- Hardiness zones describe winter cold, not planting dates.
- Planting more than can be watered during a holiday.

## Done when
Each crop has dates derived from a cited local reference and a bed or container with adequate sun and spacing.
