# Hacked Account Takeback

## When to use
A personal account shows unknown sign-ins, sent messages, changed details or a lockout.

## Steps
1. Reach the provider's recovery page by typing its address or from the app. The owner enters all credentials and codes.
2. Use a trusted, updated device. If password-stealing software is suspected, clean or avoid the original device first, or new passwords and sessions may be taken again.
3. If the email that resets this account is also affected, recover the email first.
4. Once in: set a new unique password, sign out all sessions, then enable strong two-step verification.
5. Hunt for persistence: changed recovery details, added two-step methods, mail forwarding and filters that hide messages, app passwords, connected third-party apps, delegates, linked logins.
6. Check damage: sent and deleted items, orders, saved payment methods, data downloads. Call the bank about charges.
7. Change that password anywhere it was reused.
8. Draft a short warning to contacts for the owner to send. Report the incident and keep timestamps.

## Watch out for
- Changing only the password while a forwarding rule or connected app keeps access.
- "Account recovery experts" in replies and direct messages; they are scams.
- Repeated failed recovery attempts from new locations; try from a familiar device and network.
- Deleting evidence needed for reports.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The owner controls the account, every persistence item has been checked, and reused passwords and linked money are dealt with.
