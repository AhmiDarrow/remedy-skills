# Design Brief

## When to use
A project needs a shared page that designers, requesters and approvers agree on before anyone draws.

## Steps
1. Read what already exists: request, research, analytics, previous designs. Do not ask for what the material answers.
2. Rewrite a requested solution ("we need a new dashboard") as the problem: who struggles, in what situation, what it costs them or the business. Keep the requested solution as an assumption to test.
3. Separate evidence from belief, naming the source of each fact.
4. Describe the audience by context of use: device, frequency, expertise, pressure, assistive technology and language needs.
5. State goals and non-goals. Non-goals should name tempting things that are out.
6. List constraints as fixed or negotiable: deadline and why it exists, budget, brand, platform, technical limits, legal review, accessibility level.
7. Define success as observable results with a baseline, target and when and how it will be measured. If there is no baseline, make getting one a task.
8. Name deliverables with format and fidelity, and what is not delivered.
9. Set milestones with the feedback wanted at each, number of revision rounds, who gives input and the one person who decides.
10. End with open questions and risks, each with an owner. Keep it to about two pages and ask the decision owner to confirm it.

## Watch out for
- "Modern, clean, intuitive" constrains nothing; ask for examples and what they must not feel like.
- A metric the design cannot plausibly move, such as total revenue alone.
- Filling unknown budgets or dates with plausible guesses. Write unknown.

## Operating rules
- Look before you change anything: read the real files, data or page first.
- Report what you actually ran and saw; never invent results, sources or numbers.
- Ask before destructive, paid, public or irreversible actions.
- Never print, store or commit secrets or private personal data.

## Done when
The brief states problem, audience, evidence, non-goals, constraints, measurable success, deliverables, decision owner and open questions, with every guess labelled and sign-off requested.
